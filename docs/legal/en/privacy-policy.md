# GOTCHA Privacy Policy

> This English text is a translation provided for convenience. The Hebrew version of this document is the authoritative one, and in case of any conflict or inconsistency between the versions, the Hebrew version prevails.

Effective date: July 18, 2026

## 1. Who we are

This policy is issued by GOTCHA by Omer Serruya, the operator of gotcha.co.il ("GOTCHA", "we", "us").

GOTCHA is an AI-powered customer engagement platform: businesses connect their messaging channels (WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook Messenger, email, Slack, voice) and GOTCHA provides a shared team inbox, AI assistants ("AI employees"), knowledge-base answers, and CRM/commerce integrations.

For privacy matters, contact us at privacy@gotcha.co.il. For general support, contact support@gotcha.co.il.

## 2. Our roles: controller and processor

GOTCHA serves businesses ("customers" or "tenants"). Those businesses use GOTCHA to communicate with their own customers ("end-customers").

- For end-customer data (messages, contact details, conversation content, call data, CRM records processed through the platform), our customer, the business, is the data controller. GOTCHA acts as a data processor, processing that data only to provide the service to the customer.
- For platform account data (the business's staff user accounts, waitlist and lead information, billing contact details), GOTCHA is the data controller.

If you are an end-customer of a business that uses GOTCHA and you wish to exercise your privacy rights, the business you communicated with is the controller of your data. You can contact them directly, or contact us at privacy@gotcha.co.il and we will assist and, where appropriate, refer your request to the relevant business.

## 3. What we collect

### 3a. Customer and account information

When a business signs up for or uses GOTCHA, we process:

- Staff user accounts: email address, name, phone number, role, preferred language, and account status.
- Organization details: business name, workspace settings, default country and locale, and security policy settings (such as MFA requirements).
- Team invitations: invitee email address and assigned role.
- Waitlist and lead information submitted through our public waitlist form: first name, email, phone, company, role, company size, and free-text notes.
- Billing contact details and payment metadata (see section 3h and section 6). We never store full card numbers; cards are tokenized by our payment provider.

Passwords, MFA secrets, and sign-in sessions are held by our self-hosted identity service running on our own infrastructure. The GOTCHA application stores no passwords, ever, and administrators can never see or set another user's password.

### 3b. End-customer information (our customers' customers)

When end-customers communicate with a business through GOTCHA, we process on that business's behalf:

- Contact records: name, phone number, email address, avatar, channel identifiers (such as a WhatsApp phone number or a Messenger/Instagram ID), tags, custom fields, and marketing opt-out status.
- Conversation and message content: the full text of messages exchanged, sender names, attached media references, and message metadata.
- Conversation intelligence generated by the platform: summaries, detected sentiment, detected intent, topics, and structured facts extracted from conversations (with the supporting quote recorded as evidence).
- Customer profiles and opportunity records: consolidated details such as phone, email, display name, behavioral signals, and deal-related facts, including links to records in a CRM the business has connected.
- Meeting bookings: the end-customer's email address, the scheduled time, timezone, and meeting join link, when the business uses scheduling features.

### 3c. Technical information

We process technical data needed to operate and secure the platform:

- Sign-in and session records held by our self-hosted identity service, including login history (available to each user in their account page) and active session listings, which include IP address and browser user-agent information.
- Audit logs of significant actions in the platform (user and tenant lifecycle changes, role and permission changes, credential and integration changes, consent and data-request events), scoped per tenant.
- AI usage metering records: model used, feature, token counts, and cost. These metering records contain counts and costs only; they do not store the text of prompts or responses.
- Traffic metadata processed by our network provider (Cloudflare) in the course of DNS, TLS, and DDoS protection.

We do not run any analytics, telemetry, or tracking SDKs of our own. There is no Google Analytics, no advertising pixel, and no error-reporting service in the product.

### 3d. AI interactions

GOTCHA's AI features are powered by OpenAI, our only AI provider. The content we send to OpenAI is limited to:

- conversation text needed to generate a reply or a summary;
- knowledge-base text, for generating embeddings used in retrieval;
- business profile text provided during onboarding.

No audio is sent to OpenAI. AI configuration created by the business (such as an AI employee's instructions, goals, and persona) is stored on our infrastructure so the AI features can operate as configured. See section 5 for details on AI processing and section 8 for international transfers.

### 3e. Uploaded files and media

Files sent through connected messaging channels (for example, images or documents received on WhatsApp) are stored on GOTCHA-managed storage in our hosting environment, and the platform stores a reference to the stored file. Documents uploaded to the knowledge base are parsed into text; the extracted text and its search vectors are stored on our infrastructure, and the search vectors are held in a self-hosted vector database that we operate.

### 3f. Voice and call data

Voice features use telephony provided through the customer's own Twilio account (the customer brings its own Twilio credentials). When voice is enabled, we process:

- call session records: caller and agent phone numbers, call identifiers, call state, and duration;
- call analysis: transcript-derived state frames, rolling summaries, and final call summaries;
- recording references: when the customer enables call recording, the recording audio resides with Twilio, and GOTCHA stores the recording reference and the call analysis, not the audio file.

Speech-to-text is optional and per tenant: call audio is sent to a speech-to-text provider (Deepgram or Google Cloud Speech) only if the voice channel is enabled and that provider has been configured for the tenant. In the default configuration, call audio is sent to no speech-to-text provider.

### 3g. Cookies and similar technologies

GOTCHA sets no cookies of its own. Our self-hosted sign-in service sets strictly necessary session and CSRF cookies on the sign-in subdomain. We use no analytics, marketing, or tracking cookies at all. The application keeps sign-in tokens and interface preferences in your browser's localStorage. Full details, including a table of localStorage items, are in our Cookie Policy: see ./cookie-policy.md.

### 3h. Billing information

Payments are processed by iCount, an Israeli billing provider. We process billing contact details, subscription and invoice records, and payment metadata. Cards are tokenized by the payment provider; GOTCHA never stores full card numbers.

## 4. Why we process data and legal bases

Where GOTCHA is the controller (platform account data), we rely on the following legal bases:

- Performance of a contract: creating and administering accounts, providing the platform, sending service and onboarding emails, billing and invoicing.
- Legitimate interests: securing the platform (authentication, audit logging, abuse prevention), operating and improving the service, responding to support requests, and internal operational alerting (for example, notifying our team of a new waitlist signup).
- Consent: where we rely on it, for example when you join our waitlist and ask to be contacted. You may withdraw consent at any time by contacting privacy@gotcha.co.il.
- Legal obligation: tax and accounting record-keeping connected to invoicing, and responding to lawful requests from authorities.

Where GOTCHA is the processor (end-customer data), we process only on the documented instructions of the controller, our customer, as set out in our data processing terms. The business, as controller, is responsible for establishing its own legal basis for the communications it conducts through GOTCHA.

## 5. AI processing

- Our only AI provider is OpenAI, used for text generation (replies, summaries, agent assistance, onboarding conversation) and text embeddings (knowledge-base retrieval).
- We send OpenAI only the content described in section 3d. We do not send call audio or any other audio to OpenAI.
- AI usage is metered and logged (model, feature, token counts, cost) for billing and audit purposes; these logs do not contain the prompt or response text.
- AI outputs can be inaccurate. Businesses using GOTCHA remain responsible for reviewing AI behavior and outputs in their use of the platform.

## 6. Payments

Payments are processed by iCount, an Israeli payment and invoicing provider. iCount tokenizes payment cards; GOTCHA receives and stores only payment metadata and the token references needed to manage subscriptions and issue invoices. GOTCHA never stores full card numbers.

## 7. Sharing your data

We do not sell personal data. We share data only as described below.

### 7a. Subprocessors chosen by GOTCHA

These providers process data on our infrastructure choices and can touch data across tenants:

| Provider | Purpose | Data | Region |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon Web Services (AWS) | Hosting, storage, backups | All platform data | il-central-1 (Israel) |
| Cloudflare | DNS, TLS, DDoS protection, network tunnel | Traffic metadata; content in transit | Global edge |
| OpenAI | AI text generation and embeddings | Conversation text, knowledge-base text, business profile text | United States |
| iCount | Payment processing and invoicing | Billing contact details, payment metadata (tokenized cards) | Israel |
| Email delivery provider (SMTP; currently Gmail/Google Workspace) | Transactional email | Recipient address, email content | Global (Google) |
| Deepgram (optional, only if configured for a tenant's voice channel) | Speech-to-text | Call audio | United States |
| Google Cloud Speech (optional, only if configured for a tenant's voice channel) | Speech-to-text | Call audio | Per Google Cloud configuration |

In addition, GOTCHA uses Telegram solely for our own internal operational alerts: when someone joins our public waitlist, an alert carrying the lead's name and contact details is sent to an internal GOTCHA channel. Telegram is not used for customer data processing at the tenant level.

Our identity service (Authentik), our vector database (Qdrant), and our databases (Postgres, Redis) are self-hosted on infrastructure GOTCHA manages and are not third-party processors.

The current subprocessor list is maintained at ./subprocessors.md.

### 7b. Tenant-connected integrations

The following integrations are controlled by the business using GOTCHA. Data flows to and from them only when, and only because, the business connects them. They act as data sources and recipients under the customer's instruction, not as GOTCHA-chosen subprocessors:

- Messaging channels: Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, Instagram, Facebook Messenger, Microsoft Outlook, Slack, Gmail (as an email channel).
- CRM and commerce connectors, including HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho CRM, monday, Fireberry, Airtable, Shopify, WooCommerce, Stripe, PayPal, ReturnGo, and customer-managed databases.
- Knowledge-base sources: Google Drive and Confluence.
- Scheduling: Google Calendar and Calendly.
- Voice telephony: Twilio, using the customer's own Twilio account and credentials.

When a business connects an integration, GOTCHA reads and writes data with that provider as instructed by the business (for example, reading customer or order records from a connected CRM or commerce platform, or writing an appointment to a connected calendar). The business's relationship with each provider is governed by that provider's own terms and privacy policy.

### 7c. Google user data and the Google API Services User Data Policy

Some tenant-connected integrations access Google user data through Google APIs, only when the business connects the relevant Google account:

- Google Calendar (calendar and calendar events scopes): to check availability and create, update, or cancel appointments as part of scheduling features.
- Google Drive (read-only scope): to sync files the business selects into its knowledge base.
- Gmail (a single scope, `gmail.modify`): to operate Gmail as a connected support email channel, receiving and sending messages in the shared inbox. The connected mailbox address is read from the Gmail profile under that same scope, so no separate Google identity scope is requested.

GOTCHA's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically, we use Google user data only to provide the connected feature the business enabled; we do not use it for advertising; we do not sell it; and we do not transfer it to third parties except as necessary to provide those features, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger or acquisition with prior notice. Human access to Google user data is limited to what is necessary for security, compliance, support at the customer's request, or as required by law.

### 7d. Meta platform data

WhatsApp, Instagram, and Messenger conversations are processed through Meta's platforms under the business's own Meta assets and permissions. We process Meta platform data (messages, sender identifiers, media) only to provide the messaging inbox and automation features to the business that connected the channel, consistent with Meta's Platform Terms and applicable Meta developer policies. We do not use Meta platform data for advertising and do not sell it.

### 7e. Shopify and commerce customer data

Where a business connects Shopify or another commerce platform, we access customer and order data only as needed to provide the connected feature (for example, answering an end-customer's question about their order, or supporting a return flow). Consistent with Shopify's protected customer data requirements, we apply data minimization and purpose limitation: we request and process only the order and customer fields required for the feature in use, we use them solely for that purpose, and we do not use commerce customer data for advertising or unrelated profiling.

### 7f. Other disclosures

We may disclose data where required by law or a valid legal process, or to protect the rights, safety, and security of GOTCHA, our customers, or the public. We will assess any such request carefully and disclose no more than is required.

## 8. International transfers

Primary hosting and storage are in the AWS il-central-1 region in Israel, on infrastructure GOTCHA manages, with Cloudflare in front for DNS, TLS, and DDoS protection, and backups stored in S3 in the same account. Israel benefits from a European Commission adequacy decision for transfers from the EEA.

Some subprocessors process data outside Israel:

- OpenAI processes conversation, knowledge-base, and business profile text in the United States.
- Deepgram (if configured by the tenant) processes call audio in the United States.
- Google (email delivery, and Google Cloud Speech where configured) processes data per its global or configured infrastructure.
- Cloudflare processes traffic at its global edge.

For these transfers we rely on each provider's data processing terms, which include commitments for international transfers, and we limit what we send to each provider to the data listed in section 7a.

## 9. Security

We apply the following measures, all of which are implemented in the platform today:

- Single sign-on via OpenID Connect against our self-hosted identity service, with multi-factor authentication support (authenticator apps, passkeys, and recovery codes) and per-tenant MFA enforcement policies. The GOTCHA application stores no passwords.
- Fail-closed authentication: if identity cannot be verified, access is denied rather than allowed.
- Role-based access control and tenant isolation enforced at the data layer, so each business's data is scoped to that business.
- Encryption in transit via TLS for traffic to the platform.
- AES-256-GCM encryption for stored credentials and integration secrets (channel tokens, API keys, calendar tokens, voice credentials).
- Audit logging of significant account, permission, integration, and data-request events.
- Nightly backups, encrypted in transit, to S3 in the same AWS account and region, with defined expiry (see section 10).
- Single-region hosting in AWS il-central-1 (Israel).

No system is perfectly secure, and we do not promise absolute security. If we become aware of a personal data breach affecting a customer for whom we act as processor, we will notify that customer without undue delay.

## 10. Retention

- Conversation and platform data has no fixed default retention period: it is retained while the customer's account is active, so the business can operate its inbox and history.
- Businesses can configure per-tenant retention policies for messages, AI usage logs, and audit logs, defining how long each category is kept.
- Erasure requests are fulfilled by hard deletion: the data is deleted, not anonymized or masked.
- Backups: nightly database dumps, encrypted in transit, are stored in S3. A lifecycle rule moves backups to infrequent-access storage after 30 days and deletes them at 90 days. Disk snapshots are kept for 7 days. Data deleted from production therefore leaves all backups within at most 90 days.
- Billing records may be retained longer where required by tax and accounting law.

## 11. Your rights

Depending on applicable law, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, export, restrict, or object to the processing of your personal data, and to withdraw consent where processing is based on consent. You may also lodge a complaint with your data protection authority.

How rights are fulfilled on GOTCHA:

- If you are a staff user or end-customer of a business using GOTCHA, that business is the controller of your data. Its administrators have built-in tools in the platform to export and erase user accounts and end-customer contact records (including conversation content for contacts) and to record consent. Contact the business first; it can act on your request directly.
- You can also email privacy@gotcha.co.il. Where GOTCHA is the processor, we will refer your request to the relevant business and assist them in fulfilling it. Where GOTCHA is the controller (for example, waitlist or billing contact data), we will handle your request directly.
- There is no self-service data-subject portal. Requests are handled by the tenant administrator's built-in tools or by our team via email, and identity verification is performed manually by the operator (for example, by matching the requester to the account email or working through the controller).
- We will respond to requests without undue delay and within one month, extendable where applicable law permits for complex requests.

To request deletion by email, write to privacy@gotcha.co.il with the subject line "Data Deletion Request" and include the phone number or email address associated with your data, so that we can locate the relevant records. If you contacted a business through WhatsApp or another channel, you can also ask that business directly, and its administrators can delete the conversation and contact record from their own dashboard.

Exports are provided in a structured, machine-readable format (JSON). Erasure is a hard delete, and deleted data leaves backups within at most 90 days as described in section 10.

## 12. Children
